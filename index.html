About & Positionality

Marianna’s research examines how institutional and cultural contexts shape belonging, support, and wellbeing for marginalized students, with a focus on the transition to college. She studies sources of support including family, faculty, community, and emerging technologies, as well as the responsibility institutions hold in making those supports accessible, particularly for Latiné, LGBTQ+, and other students at the intersections of marginalization. As a queer, neurodivergent, Latiné scholar, her work is grounded in lived experience and a commitment to challenging deficit framings of students, creating collaborative research that validates student stories, and informing more equitable campus practice.

Selected Research & Projects

Latiné Student Adjustment & Institutional Responsibility at HSIs Project Lead

Conducting empirical studies applying LatCrit frameworks to examine family support (familismo), professor impact, social adjustment, and mental health among Latiné college students at Hispanic-Serving Institutions.

Human-AI Relationships & Moral Perception Project Co-Lead / GRA

Investigating how LGBTQ+ young adults utilize AI chatbots for companionship and emotional support using AI photovoice methods, as well as multi-institution quantitative analysis on higher education students' moral beliefs and learning behaviors regarding AI.

California Healthy Youth Act (AB 329) Policy Analysis Project Lead

Utilizing Lipsky’s street-level bureaucracy theory and queer theory to examine the implementation of California's LGBTQ+-inclusive sex education mandates in public secondary schools.

Intersectional Stress & Wellbeing in Sexual Minority Youth GRA

Examining associations between intersectional oppression-based stress, PTSS, suicidality, and protective social factors among national samples of sexual minority adolescents and young adults of color.

Peer-Reviewed Publications

  • Exploring the College Adjustment of Latiné Students at Hispanic Serving Institutions
    Amato, M., Lazarevic, V., & Shapiro, A. (2024)
    Journal of Underrepresented and Minority Progress, 8(SI-2), 78–98.
    Key Finding: Familismo acts as a protective factor directly associated with positive academic adjustment and lower depressive symptoms, highlighting how faculty can mirror familismo values to enhance student support at HSIs.
  • Oppression-Based Stressors, Posttraumatic Stress Symptoms, and Self-Injurious Thoughts and Behaviors Among a National Sample of Queer Adolescents of Color
    Sullivan, T. R., Amato, M., Goldbach, J. T., & Mereish, E. H. (2026)
    Journal of Traumatic Stress, 39(1), 45–58.
    Key Finding: Examined how unique, intersectional oppression-based stressors at the convergence of heterosexism and racism serve as significant risk factors for trauma-related symptoms and self-harm among queer BIPOC youth.
  • How Intimate Partner Violence Is Influenced by Social Identity Among Sexual Minority Men
    Jessup, O., Nacht, C. L., Amato, M., Reynolds, H. E., Felner, J. K., Hong, C., ... & Storholm, E. D. (2024)
    LGBT Health, 11(6), 465–474.
    Key Finding: Identified how social identity power dynamics (such as immigration status, race, and outness) and internalized homophobia shape partner violence, calling for intersectional intervention models.
  • The Association Between Social Support and Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis Use Among Sexual Minority Men in the United States: A Scoping Literature Review
    Nacht, C. L., Reynolds, H. E., Jessup, O., Amato, M., & Storholm, E. D. (2024)
    AIDS and Behavior, 28(11), 3559–3573.
    Key Finding: Summarized national evidence on how distinct types and sources of social support (including chosen family and gay community attachment) influence PrEP adoption and adherence.
