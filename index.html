About & Research Statement

Marianna’s research examines how institutional and cultural contexts shape belonging, support, and wellbeing for marginalized students, with a focus on the transition to college. She studies the sources of support including family, faculty, community, and emerging technologies, and the responsibility institutions hold in making those supports accessible, particularly for Latiné, LGBTQ+, and other students at the intersections of marginalization.

Her community-engaged inquiry seeks to understand both broad patterns in student experience and how institutional policies play out in practice. Her work is grounded in intersectionality and critical frameworks, including LatCrit and queer theory, to challenge deficit framings of students and inform more equitable campus practice. She also mentors undergraduate researchers and prospective graduate applicants and has supported college access programming in San Diego since 2021.

Selected Research & Projects

Latiné Student Adjustment & Institutional Responsibility at HSIs Project Lead

Conducting empirical studies applying LatCrit frameworks to examine family support (familismo), professor impact, social adjustment, and mental health among Latiné college students at Hispanic-Serving Institutions.

Human-AI Relationships & Moral Perception Project Co-Lead / GRA

Investigating how LGBTQ+ young adults utilize AI chatbots for companionship and emotional support using AI photovoice methods, as well as multi-institution quantitative analysis on higher education students' moral beliefs and learning behaviors regarding AI.

California Healthy Youth Act (AB 329) Policy Analysis Project Lead

Utilizing Lipsky’s street-level bureaucracy theory and queer theory to examine the uneven implementation of California's LGBTQ+-inclusive sex education mandates in public secondary schools.

Intersectional Stress & Wellbeing in Sexual Minority Youth GRA

Examining associations between intersectional oppression-based stress, PTSS, suicidality, and protective social factors among national samples of sexual minority adolescents and young adults of color.
